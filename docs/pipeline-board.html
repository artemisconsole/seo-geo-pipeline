NEW · GEO Brand & GEO · attribute thinking Before prompts and content, find the brand's standout attribute. In AI search, the more unique an attribute, the more readily AI recommends the brand for it — so this decides the prompt set, the entity audit and the content angle.
Root low diff.

Every competitor has it — "for families", "for business", "in <city>". Table stakes; hard to get cited for it alone.

Rare med diff.

Harder to match — "luxury", "private beach", "boutique". A real edge over most rivals.

Unique high diff.

One of a kind — "under-water hotel". Highest differentiation → AI recommends it most readily.

Pick the standout attribute the brand wants to own — e.g. "luxury beachfront in Phu Quoc". That single decision drives: the prompt set in the Plan (Tracking tab), the Entity Audit (is that attribute consistently tied to the brand on-site and across the web?), and the content angle (writing for a luxury guest ≠ a business guest). It also shapes the Brand Guideline (/brand-init) the writers use.
PIVOTAL ★ P04f · Quick Win & Win Strategy The plan says what to do; this decides what to do first for fast results, and the strategy to win — hit KPI and drive bookings, submits, revenue.

A · DO FIRST Quick wins (high impact, low effort)

  • Keyword cannibalisation — merge/route competing URLs, pick the money page
  • Striking distance — positions 5–20 with intent, push into the top 3
  • Money-page on-page — title/H1/meta/schema/CTA on service & booking pages
  • Technical quick-fix — index blockers, speed, missing schema on revenue pages
  • GBP / local — complete profile, posts, fix NAP

B · WIN Winning strategy (path to KPI + revenue)

  • Topical coverage — own the whole problem-pattern cluster
  • Template / programmatic — best-time-to-visit, food guide, best-hotel-near-X
  • Local SEO — GBP, local landing pages, reviews, map pack
  • Backlinks / authority — digital PR, lift GEO citations (cost → L approves)
  • GEO coverage — answer chunks, entities, the standout attribute
